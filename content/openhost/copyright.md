# OpenHost Copyright and Abuse Policy

Draft updated October 2, 2026. Effective date: pending adoption. This is a proposed process for a developing hosting service. Publishing it does not establish a registered copyright agent, an operational takedown program or eligibility for a statutory safe harbor.

## Current contact and agent status

Send concerns to [legal@digifender.com](mailto:legal@digifender.com). Digifender's [company contact page](/legal/copyright) states that no DMCA designated-agent registration has been filed for Digifender LLC. The contact below is a reporting contact, not a representation that the required designation for OpenHost has been completed. A business registered agent is not automatically a copyright agent.

Before relying on the US Digital Millennium Copyright Act notice-and-takedown framework, Digifender must confirm the applicable service category, complete any required agent designation, publish matching contact details and operate the required procedures. The [US Copyright Office overview](https://www.copyright.gov/512/) explains those conditions; [17 USC Section 512](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap5.html#512) supplies the statutory requirements.

## Copyright reports

A copyright owner or authorized representative can report a concern using the legal mailbox. A formal notice should identify the protected work, the allegedly infringing material and enough location information to find it; provide contact information and a signature; and contain the required statements of good-faith belief, accuracy and authority. Consider licenses, fair use and other lawful permissions before submitting a claim.

Use the Copyright Office's official notice guidance for the required statements. Knowingly making a material misrepresentation can have legal consequences. A report should identify actual material or activity rather than ask us to remove an entire unrelated service.

## Proposed handling and counter-notices

The proposed process would assess a sufficiently detailed notice, act expeditiously where the applicable framework requires it, and notify the affected customer where appropriate. Missing information may require clarification. A complaint alone is not a final legal determination of infringement.

A customer who believes material was removed by mistake or misidentification may submit a counter-notice. It must identify the removed material and former location, include a signature and contact information, and provide the required sworn good-faith statement, jurisdiction consent and agreement to accept service. A counter-notice can expose the sender to litigation; consult the official guidance before submitting one.

Under the Section 512 counter-notice procedure, the proposed process would send the counter-notice to the original claimant and restore qualifying material within 10 to 14 business days after receipt unless notified of a filed court action seeking to restrain the infringement. An independent lawful reason to restrict a service must be explained separately. This statutory framework is not a promise that OpenHost has already established every condition for it.

## Repeated infringement and other abuse

Before launch, Digifender must adopt and reasonably implement an appropriate repeat-infringer policy, accommodate applicable standard technical measures and train staff to assess reports and disputes. Repeated infringement may result in account termination under the adopted policy. A disputed or mistaken allegation should not automatically be counted as an established violation.

Non-copyright concerns, including phishing, malware, impersonation and privacy abuse, can use the same mailbox under the [Acceptable Use Policy](/legal/openhost/acceptable-use). You do not need to own copyright to report another type of harm.

## Information sharing and contact

Reports, counter-notices and relevant identifying information may need to be shared with the other party, necessary providers, advisers or authorities for investigation, the applicable legal process or a legal obligation. Send only relevant information. Do not attach illegal imagery, malware, secrets or unnecessary sensitive records.
